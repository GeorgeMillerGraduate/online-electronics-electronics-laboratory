Jenga Code / engineering notes

The engines behind the instruments.

The diagrams, wiring editor, digital models, teaching assembler and interface were created for this laboratory. The analogue solver and Z80 instruction engine build on open-source work.

Analogue calculations

EEcircuit-engine 1.8.0 by Danial Chitnis and contributors supplies an unmodified ngspice 45.2 WebAssembly build. Analyses execute in your browser worker; circuits are not sent to a simulation server.

The EEcircuit wrapper is MIT-licensed. Ngspice and its linked components have their own licences, including BSD and LGPL notices. See the ngspice licensing notice, complete attribution and included ngspice source.

Z80 computer

Lawrence Kesteloot’s Z80 emulator 2.3.0 and z80-base 2.0.1 are MIT-licensed. The computer shows instruction-level memory and I/O operations, registers, RAM and two output ports. It is a fixed-board computer, not a user-wireable physical Z80 IC.

What the models mean

Digital ICs use original functional models. Supply pins must be connected, and undriven/conflicting states are reported. Propagation delays, loading, current limits and transistor physics are not part of these digital models.

The analogue bench uses SPICE calculations. Its diode and transistor defaults are generic teaching models; the op-amp is ideal and unsaturated. The two domains run separately. Synchronized mixed-signal simulation, programmable bus wiring and additional CPU families are not implemented in this edition.
